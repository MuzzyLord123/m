We were asked to paint three small offices. By the time we had finished those,
they had us do the rest of the office complex, and then the warehouse as well.

That is the part worth saying out loud: nothing stopped while we were there. The
offices were in use, the warehouse was still moving stock, and very little of
what was in the way could actually be moved. We took it a room at a time, sheeted
what stayed, cut in around it by hand, and handed each area back finished before
we opened up the next one. Nobody was sent home and nobody worked in a room with
a dust sheet over their desk for a fortnight.

The specification was chosen for the traffic rather than the photograph — a blue
feature wall, and a durable white matt through the rest of it that will take
being scuffed and wiped down for years without going patchy. A commercial
building does not need a delicate finish. It needs one that still looks like this
after everybody has spent two winters dragging things past it.
