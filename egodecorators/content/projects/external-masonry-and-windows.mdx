The frames were the real job. From the path it looked like the house wanted
painting; up close the paint had already gone at the joints and the bottoms of
the frames were soft where water had been sitting in the end grain. Painting
over that would have looked well until about the second winter.

So the timber came first. We cut back past the rot until the chisel was biting
into something hard, made the sections good, and primed the bare wood and every
cut end the same day we sanded it. Only then did anything decorative go on.

The walls we kept close to the colour they already were — the house did not need
to become a different house, it needed to stop looking neglected. The frames
went a warm brown, which is what lifts the whole elevation in the after shot.
Most of what you are looking at, though, is a fortnight of preparation you
cannot see.
