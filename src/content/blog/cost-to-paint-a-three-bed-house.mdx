Three decorators will give you three different numbers for the same house, and the
spread is usually wider than people expect. Here is what sits behind those numbers,
using the prices we are actually quoting around {site.town} this year.

## The short answer

A full interior repaint of a typical three-bedroom semi — walls, ceilings and woodwork
throughout, in sound condition — lands between **£3,200 and £5,500**. Most come in
around £4,000.

That covers roughly nine spaces: three bedrooms, a lounge, a kitchen or diner, a
bathroom, plus the hall, stairs and landing. It assumes two full coats everywhere and
water-based satin on the woodwork.

If you only want the walls done and the woodwork left alone, take about a third off.
If the house has been neglected for fifteen years, add a week of preparation and
expect the top of that range or beyond.

## What actually moves the price

Paint is a small part of the cost. On a £4,000 job, materials are usually £450 to
£700. The rest is labour, and labour is mostly preparation.

### Condition of the walls

This is the biggest single variable. Sound, previously painted plaster takes filler in
a few places and sands in an hour. Walls with blown plaster, old textured coating, or
four layers of wallpaper underneath can add three or four days to the job before any
paint is opened.

### Ceilings

Ceilings are slow. They are overhead work, they show every roller mark, and artex or
water staining means a stain blocker first. A house with coving in every room takes
noticeably longer than one without, because the edge has to be cut in by hand.

### Woodwork

Skirting, architrave, doors, window boards and a staircase are where the hours go. A
door done properly is rubbed down, dusted off, cut in around the ironmongery and
coated twice. Reckon on two to three hours a door, both sides.

If the existing paint is old oil-based gloss that has gone yellow and brittle, it needs
more than a light sand. That is a real cost and an honest quote will say so.

### Access and living arrangements

An empty house is quicker than an occupied one. Working around furniture, pets and
someone on a work call adds time to every room. It is entirely normal, and worth
saying up front so it is priced rather than discovered.

| Double bedroom | £340 – £460 | £520 – £700 |
| Lounge | £400 – £550 | £620 – £850 |
| Hall, stairs and landing | £550 – £900 | £850 – £1,300 |
| Bathroom | £220 – £320 | £340 – £480 |

Hall, stairs and landing is always the one that surprises people. It is a tall,
awkward space needing scaffold boards or a stair ladder, and it has more woodwork per
square metre than any other part of the house.

## Why one quote comes in much lower

A quote a thousand pounds under the others is usually explained by one of four things.
None of them are dishonest, but you should know which one you are buying.

- **One coat instead of two.** It looks fine for about eight months, particularly over
  a colour change, and then the old colour starts to grin through.
- **Trade paint at contract grade.** Contract matt is designed for new builds handed
  over quickly. It marks easily and cannot be wiped.
- **Preparation skipped.** No caulking to the skirting, filler left proud, nothing
  sanded between coats.
- **Cash, no insurance, no guarantee.** Fine until something goes wrong on your stairs.

Ask each decorator the same question: how many coats, on what preparation, in which
paint. The answers usually explain the gap in about a minute.

## Getting a number for your house

Photographs get you a close estimate. A twenty-minute visit gets you an exact one,
because we measure up and look at the things photographs hide, such as the state of
the woodwork and whether the ceilings have been papered.

Either way the price is itemised, so you can see what the preparation costs and decide
what you want doing. Nothing on the invoice will be a surprise.
