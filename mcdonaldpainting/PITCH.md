# The current website, and what replaces it

For Sean. Written to be read without a developer sitting next to you.

Nothing in here is a criticism of you. Most of it is a criticism of a website built for a
different business than the one you now run — a decorating business, five or six years ago,
before the commercial and industrial work became the bigger half. Some of it came from an
agency you paid, and none of it is unusual. It is just out of date, and it is costing you the
enquiries you would most like to have.

---

## The one-sentence version

Your website tells a facilities manager that you are a painter and decorator. It does not tell
them you are SafeContractor approved, that you run multi-year painting contracts, that you paint
steelwork and factory floors, or that you have worked in a high school assembly hall. Those are
the things that win the work you want. The new site is built around them.

---

## Side by side

### 1. The company changed its name. The website did not finish changing.

**Now.** The header links to the correct `mcdonaldpaintingcontractors` Instagram and Facebook.
The body copy on the same page links to `mandrpaintingcontractors` — the old M & R accounts. Two
different sets of social links on one page. There is also a page still at
`/home-m-r-painting-contractors/`, and a photograph of you whose hidden description reads
"Director of **McDpnald** Painting Contractors Ltd."

**Why it matters.** None of it is visible to a casual visitor. All of it is visible to somebody
checking you out before putting you on a tender list — and the whole thing you are selling is
that you are careful.

**New.** One set of social links. The old page redirects to the About page so the link is not
lost. Every image description is written properly. Before anything is deleted at the other end,
we check whether those old accounts hold photographs that exist nowhere else.

### 2. Your email address is on a different domain to your website.

**Now.** The site is `mcdonaldpaintingcontractors.co.uk`. The email on it is
`info@mcdonaldpainting.co.uk`.

**Why it matters.** This is the one on the list that may actually be losing you money today.
Procurement teams and school business managers have spam filters that check whether the email
domain matches the website domain. A quote from an address that does not match can be held,
quarantined, or read as a phishing attempt — and nobody tells you it happened.

**New.** The site shows the phone number and a contact form until you confirm which address is
canonical and that mail on it is definitely being read. It is the first question in
`CONTENT-NEEDED.md`.

### 3. The menu tells a buyer nothing about what you do.

**Now.** Eight tabs: Home · About Us · Projects Gallery · Health & Safety · Blog · FAQ ·
Testimonials · Contact. Not one of them names a building type or a capability.

**Why it matters.** A facilities manager with a factory can read your entire navigation and
still not know whether you paint factories.

**New.** A numbered index of the eight sectors you work in — Education, Healthcare, Industrial
& manufacturing, Offices & commercial, Hospitality & retail, Programmed maintenance, Steelwork
& specialist coatings, Residential. Each one is a real page written for the person who looks
after that kind of building. They are also eight new ways for someone to find you in a search
that names a building rather than a trade.

### 4. The most recent thing your website says is about paint colours from 2020.

**Now.** The latest blog post is "4 tips for incorporating Dulux's Colour of the Year 2020 into
your hallway."

**Why it matters.** Five-year-old advice about hallways, on the website of a firm chasing
industrial contracts. A buyer reads it as a domestic decorator, whatever the rest of the site
says. It also makes the site look abandoned.

**New.** The blog is retired and every post redirects to a relevant page, so nothing 404s. If
you want to publish again later it should be write-ups of jobs, not colour trends — they are
worth more and they never go out of date.

### 5. SafeContractor approval gets one sentence.

**Now.** It is mentioned once, in the middle of a paragraph, on the Health & Safety page.

**Why it matters.** It is the only third-party accreditation you hold and it is the first thing
a compliance team looks for. Buried in a sentence, it does nothing.

**New.** A compliance page written to be forwarded — a buyer sends the link to their compliance
team and it answers the pre-qualification questionnaire without a phone call. SafeContractor with
Alcumus named as the assessing body, insurance limits, RAMS, COSHH, asbestos, working at height,
waste, and how you work in an occupied building. Every figure we do not yet have is shown as an
outstanding question rather than filled in with something plausible — see §7 below.

### 6. A gallery of photographs is not evidence.

**Now.** "Projects Gallery" is photographs with no information attached.

**Why it matters.** A buyer looking at a painting contractor's photographs is trying to answer
one question: have they done this, in a building like mine, while it was still in use. A
photograph cannot answer that.

**New.** Site records. Each one carries the same seven fields — sector, client type, scope,
location, duration, coating system, and whether the building stayed open — so two jobs can be
compared without reading two paragraphs. The high school assembly hall, the Willaston shopfront
and the factory and warehouse work are drafted. They need about ten minutes of your time each to
finish, and that is the single highest-value thing you can do for this website.

### 7. Where a fact is missing, the site says so.

You will see yellow-marked boxes on the new site saying **CONFIRM** — insurance limits, the
SafeContractor certificate number, how many operatives you have, what year the company started.

They are deliberate. They are the questions we need answered, shown in the place the answer will
go, so you can see exactly what each one unlocks. Nothing has been invented to fill a gap: there
is no accreditation on this site you do not hold, no contract value, no client name, and no
number anybody has guessed at. When you answer a question, it is deleted from one file and every
marker for it disappears from the site at once.

`CONTENT-NEEDED.md` is the full list, in the order worth working through.

### 8. The words.

**Now.** "High quality" appears five times on the home page. "Painters and decorators" is in
almost every sentence. The home page finishes with "with a smile!".

**Why it matters.** That copy was written for a search engine in about 2018, and search engines
stopped rewarding it years ago. A commercial buyer reads it as a domestic decorator.

**New.** None of those phrases appear anywhere — there is an automated check that fails the build
if one gets added back. The copy is written the way you would talk to someone who has a building
to look after: specific about method, scope and compliance, quiet about quality. The site never
says the work is good. It shows you how the work is run and lets the reader decide.

### 9. The home page is a plugin.

**Now.** The top of the home page is the MasterSlider WordPress plugin — a stock carousel with an
empty placeholder image as its source.

**Why it matters.** Sliders are ignored by visitors and slow to load. This one is also broken.

**New.** No slider. The page opens like the cover sheet of a submission: what you are, where
you work, the accreditation, the company number, and one thing to do next — on white, in
navy, with your accreditation and company number visible before anybody scrolls.

A note on how it looks, since you will be asked. The site is white and deep navy with a
single amber accent, because that is what contractors at the top of this market look like
and it is what a facilities manager expects to find. An earlier version of this rebuild was
dark and used a neon yellow; it looked striking and it read as an architecture practice
rather than as a firm you would put on a tender list. It was redrawn.

### 10. The cookie notice is not a formality.

**Now.** The current site has no privacy notice and no consent notice that we can see.

**Why it matters.** Under UK law, analytics cookies need permission *before* they are set.
Almost every small business site gets this wrong in the same way: the tracking loads the
moment you arrive and a banner asks permission afterwards for something that has already
happened. It is the kind of thing nobody chases a painting contractor about — until a
procurement team's own compliance officer looks at your website before adding you to a
supplier list.

**New.** Nothing loads until the visitor chooses. Decline and no Google script is requested
by the page at all — we tested it in a browser and watched the network. The two buttons are
the same size, because refusing has to be as easy as agreeing. There is a proper privacy
page that says what is collected, where it goes and how to ask for it, and the table of
what gets stored on a visitor's device is generated from the same file the code reads, so
the page cannot describe something the site does not do.

## Something no other painting contractor near you will have

The new site generates a **six-page capability statement as a PDF**, on demand, from the same
words that are on the website — so the two can never disagree. Cover sheet, what you do, the
schedule of works, the eight sectors, compliance and accreditation, three site records, contact
details.

Two things it does for you:

- Anyone who downloads it leaves a **work email address**. That is the lead worth having: it is
  somebody with a building and a budget, not a passer-by.
- You get a **direct link you can paste into an email**, so when a buyer asks "have you got
  anything you can send me?", the answer takes four seconds instead of an evening.

It is generated fresh on every download. The day you confirm the insurance limits, the next
person who downloads it gets them.

## The numbers

Measured, not asserted. Google Lighthouse scores every page out of 100 for speed, accessibility,
technical quality and search readiness. Google uses these signals in ranking, and a slow site on
a phone loses enquiries before anyone reads a word.

**The new site**, every page measured on a simulated mobile phone on a slow 4G connection:

| Page                         | Speed | Accessibility | Best practices | Search |
| ---------------------------- | ----: | ------------: | -------------: | -----: |
| Home                         |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Programmed maintenance       |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Education                    |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Industrial & manufacturing   |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Capability schedule          |    97 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Compliance                   |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Site records                 |    96 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| A single site record         |    97 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| About                        |    97 |           100 |            100 |    100 |
| Contact                      |    98 |           100 |            100 |    100 |

Speed scores move by a point or two between runs — that is Lighthouse, not the site. The other
three columns do not move.

Two figures worth knowing separately:

- **Nothing on any page moves while it loads.** The measurement for that is called Cumulative
  Layout Shift and it is **0.000** on every page — the best possible. This is the thing that
  makes you press the wrong link on a phone.
- **The main text appears in about 0.75 of a second** on a throttled phone on slow 4G, measured
  in a real browser. Google's threshold for "good" is 2.5 seconds.

**The old site: not measured, and we should be straight with you about why.** The machine this
was built on has no route out to the public internet, so `mcdonaldpaintingcontractors.co.uk`
could not be loaded to test it. Running that test takes about two minutes on any laptop —
[pagespeed.web.dev](https://pagespeed.web.dev) — and it is worth doing before the meeting so the
comparison is a real number rather than our word for it. Two things are already known without
measuring: the home page loads the MasterSlider plugin, and it does so with an empty placeholder
image as the slider's source.

Nothing in this document depends on that number. Everything above is about what the site says,
not how fast it says it.

## What we have not done, and why

- **Five of the eight sector pages are shorter than the other three.** Education, Industrial and
  Programmed maintenance are written out in full, because they are where the money is and where
  we know enough to be specific. Healthcare, Offices, Hospitality & retail, Steelwork and
  Residential are real pages, honestly written, and shorter — because we would have had to pad
  them, and thin pages are what the current site already has.
- **There are three site records, not the six to eight the page is designed for.** Three more are
  shown as marked requests saying exactly what is wanted and why. That is a question for you, not
  a gap in the build.
- **There are no testimonials on the site yet.** Your current site has a testimonials page, but we
  have not been able to read it from here, and a quote has to be word for word. The two slots are
  on the page waiting.
- **Your photographs are not on it yet.** The machine this was built on cannot reach
  `mcdonaldpaintingcontractors.co.uk` — the network it sits on refuses the connection — so
  nobody here has been able to open your gallery. There is a script in the build that pulls
  every photograph off the live site at full resolution and lists them with their sizes; it
  is one command from any normal laptop. Every place a photograph belongs is already built
  and currently shows a labelled frame saying what shot is wanted.
- **Nothing has been switched over.** The current site is still live and untouched. `MIGRATION.md`
  is the order of operations for the day you decide to move, including carrying your Google
  Search Console access across so the site's search history is not lost.

## If you do only three things

1. **Send the SafeContractor certificate.** A photograph of it is fine. It unlocks the compliance
   page, the capability statement and the structured data Google reads.
2. **Ring your broker for the two insurance limits.** Public liability and employers' liability.
   One call, and it is the first question on every pre-qualification questionnaire you will ever
   be sent.
3. **Ten minutes on the phone about three jobs** — the school hall, the Willaston shopfront, and a
   factory or warehouse. When it was, who for, how long you were there, what went on the wall, and
   whether the building stayed open.

Everything else can follow.
