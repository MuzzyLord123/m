## Buying decoration as a programme rather than a scramble

Most schools buy decoration in the wrong order: whatever looks worst in July gets
done in August, and the parts of the building nobody shows to parents are
decorated when they finally become embarrassing. That is how an estate ends up
with a reception area on a three-year cycle and a science block that has not been
touched in fifteen.

A programme fixes the order. Every element is surveyed once and given a frequency
that reflects what it is and what it takes — corridors more often than
classrooms, external joinery more often than internal walls, gutters annually —
and the whole thing is then phased across the holidays so each year carries a
similar amount of work and a similar amount of money.

For a trust with several schools, the same survey produces a rotation: one
school's externals this summer, another's the next, with half terms carrying the
internal work in between. The advantage is not the paint. It is that the estates
report to the trust board says what will be done and when, for three years, and
holds.

## What an academy trust or a local authority will ask for

The pre-qualification questions are the same every time, and they are all
answerable from the compliance page: accreditation and certificate number,
insurance limits, RAMS, COSHH, safeguarding arrangements, references. Where a
framework requires a specific scheme membership we may not hold it — that is
worth establishing at the start of a conversation rather than at the end of one.

## Getting a price that survives the survey

Two things routinely make a school decoration price move after it is accepted,
and both are avoidable at survey stage. The first is rot: external joinery on an
older block usually needs more repair than it looks like it does, and finding that
out with the scaffold up is expensive. The second is the asbestos register — if it
is missing, out of date, or does not cover the element being worked on, the job
stops until it does.

We price both explicitly: repair work as a provisional sum against a schedule of
rates, and a written note of anything the register does not cover.
