## Shutdown work has to be right first time

A shutdown is the most expensive week in the plant's year and it is fully booked
before it starts. Painting in that week is only worth buying from a contractor who
will have the material on site, the access arranged and the labour resourced
before the line stops — because there is no second attempt, and a coating that has
not cured by Monday morning is a production problem, not a decorating one.

That means the survey happens well ahead, the specification is settled ahead, and
the programme is written in hours rather than days. It also means being honest at
the survey about what will not fit: a floor that needs seventy-two hours before
forklift traffic does not go into a forty-eight hour window, whatever the schedule
says.

## Steel: the specification is a preparation specification

Almost every steelwork job that fails, fails at the interface between the old
coating and the new one. What decides the outcome is the standard of preparation
and whether the new system tolerates what is already there — not the brand on the
tin.

So the questions get asked before the price: what is the existing coating and how
old is it; what cleaning grade is required, and is it achievable with hand and
power tools or does it need blasting and containment; how many coats, and what dry
film thickness is being checked against; and whether the building will be warm and
dry enough for the overcoat windows at the time of year the work is wanted. The
capability schedule sets this out in full.

## Warehouses: the work is at height and over racking

A warehouse is mostly ceiling, steel and the top of a wall, and almost all of it
sits above racking that is full. What can actually be reached, from what, with the
racking in that condition, is the first thing to establish — and it usually
determines whether the job is done in sections during operation or in a single
push over a closedown.

Where racking has to be moved or de-stocked, that is the client's cost and the
client's programme, and it is better identified at survey than discovered on the
first morning.

## Floors take the building out of use, and that is the real price

Everything about an industrial floor coating is decided by how long the area can
be closed. Preparation — grinding, shot blasting or etching — brings dust, noise
and a different amount of downtime each. The coating itself goes on quickly. The
cure is the constraint, and the difference between light foot traffic and a loaded
counterbalance forklift can be several days.

A floor is also where a moisture test earns its keep. A slab without a functioning
damp-proof membrane will lift a coating off in sheets, and it will do it about
eight months after the invoice is paid.
