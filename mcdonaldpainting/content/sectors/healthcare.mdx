## Why this sector is bought differently

Nobody appoints a painting contractor for a clinical building on price alone. The
buyer is managing a risk — that a contractor will generate dust in the wrong
place, leave an area unusable at the start of a list, or disturb something in a
building where disturbance has consequences — and they manage it by appointing
people who have done it before.

Which makes the documentation the sales material. RAMS written for the specific
ward rather than for the trade, a COSHH assessment that explains why a particular
product was chosen for an occupied area, and a clean, boring record of turning up
when you said you would.

## Care homes and primary care

Smaller buildings, the same constraints and usually no estates department to
manage it. In a care home the work happens around residents who live there, which
means noise and odour restrictions in the afternoon, no trailing leads, and
communal areas back in use before the evening. In a GP surgery or dental practice
it means working around clinic hours, which usually means evenings and weekends.
