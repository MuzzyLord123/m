## Dilapidations and hand-backs

A schedule of dilapidations is a negotiation, and the decoration in it is usually
the largest line that is genuinely disputable. Work priced against a schedule
served by a surveyor gets priced line for line against that schedule, with the
items we think are betterment rather than reinstatement identified in writing
rather than quietly included.

For a tenant approaching a break or a lease end, a survey before the schedule is
served is worth more than a price after it.

## Service charge and the annual cycle

Common parts and external cyclical decoration are funded through a service charge
that is set a year ahead, which makes this the easiest commercial work to put on a
programme and the hardest to get done reactively. A three or five-year cycle,
priced at the start, gives the agent a figure to budget and the tenants a
schedule — and it stops the decoration being the line that gets deferred every
year until it cannot be.
