## What a good steelwork enquiry contains

Four things, and with them a price can be produced that will not move:

- What the existing coating is, roughly how old it is, and what condition it is in.
- The cleaning grade required, or agreement that we survey and propose one.
- The coat build and the dry film thickness required, and how it is being verified.
- When the building will be warm and dry enough for the overcoat windows.

Without them the price is a guess dressed up as a quotation, and the difference
between the guess and the real number is usually the preparation.

## Floors: the cure is the programme

The application takes a day. The cure takes the area out of use for considerably
longer, and how much longer depends on what has to drive across it afterwards.
Every industrial floor programme should be written backwards from that number.

Moisture is the other one. A slab without a functioning damp-proof membrane will
lift a coating in sheets, about eight months after the invoice is paid, and a
moisture test before pricing is cheaper than the argument afterwards.

## Roofs: survey before you coat

A sheet roof with failed fixings, split sheets or corroded gutter lines needs those
put right first. A coating over them buys a year rather than a decade, and it
converts a roofing problem into a decorating dispute. Where a roof cannot be
safely accessed, we say so rather than pricing around it.
