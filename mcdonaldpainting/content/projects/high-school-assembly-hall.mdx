An assembly hall is the hardest room in a school to redecorate and the easiest one
to get wrong. It is tall, so everything above three metres is a tower or a scaffold
job rather than a stepladder job. It is in constant use — assemblies, exams,
performances, wet-weather lunch, parents' evenings — so the window is narrow and
fixed by a calendar that was set the previous year. And it is the room the school
shows to visitors, which means the standard expected is the standard applied to a
reception area, not a corridor.

The practical constraint is the floor. Most halls have a sprung or sealed floor
that the school is not prepared to see damaged, so protection and how plant is
moved across it is agreed before anything else. High-level work over fixed seating
or staging brings the same problem in a different form.
