Factory and warehouse work is bought on programme, not on finish. What a
production manager is buying is a contractor who can be on site while the site is
running, who will not stop the line, and who will be off the floor before the next
shift starts.

That shapes everything. Areas are taken in sections small enough to be handed back
at the end of a shift. Access plant is agreed against the racking and the traffic
routes rather than assumed. Materials are chosen for cure time and odour as much
as for finish, because a coating that needs the building empty for twenty-four
hours is not a coating that can be used in a building that runs six days a week.

Where the work is steelwork, floors or a sheet roof rather than walls, the
specification is a different document again — see the capability schedule.
